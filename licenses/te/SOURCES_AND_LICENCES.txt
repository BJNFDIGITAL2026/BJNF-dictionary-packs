WHAT BJNF TOOK AND WHAT IT CHANGED
  * Only single words and their occurrence counts were extracted. No sentence, phrase or document text
    from the corpus is stored, published or shipped. (The corpus text was collected from public web pages;
    their wording may be owned by the original publishers, so BJNF keeps only words, which are facts.)
  * Unicode NFC normalisation; only words written fully in the Telugu script were kept
    (no digits, no punctuation, no mixed scripts); stray zero-width joiners removed; length 1-40;
    obvious repeated-letter noise removed; offensive words removed (tools/blocklist/te.txt).
  * Kept words seen at least 20 times; ranked most frequent first; the top 100,000 words published.
  * English words were lower-cased.

VERIFICATION (tools/verify_words.py) - every word passed all of these checks
  1. not offensive (tools/blocklist/te.txt)
  2. valid letter sequence for the script (Unicode syllable rules)
  3. no known corruption pattern (wrong ra-ottu order, legacy-font junk)
  4. seen at least 20 times in IndicCorp v2
  5. confirmed by a second, independent source: used on te.wikipedia.org at least twice,
     or seen at least 2,000 times in IndicCorp. Wikipedia (https://dumps.wikimedia.org/tewiki/latest/tewiki-latest-pages-articles.xml.bz2) was used ONLY as a yes/no check;
     no Wikipedia text or counts are published.
  6. not a likely misspelling: a form differing by one vowel length (Hindi: or a missing anusvara)
     is 20x more common in BOTH sources; expert-checked exceptions in tools/allowlist/te.txt
  7. not on tools/rejectlist/te.txt (one missing/extra letter, found by hand review)
